The bill keeps growing

Spending per person has more than doubled since 2011.

Is health care getting more expensive?

Who pays the bill?

Families now carry a bigger share of the bill.

Whose money pays for health care?

Mostly two payers: the Ministry of Health and households paying from their own pockets.

Families' spending has grown faster, so more of the bill now falls on them.

3×growth in household out-of-pocket spending, 2011 to 2024

The bill is shifting onto families.

The Ministry of Health's share slipped, and private insurance stayed below 8%.

What does the money buy?

Most of it treats illness; little goes to prevention.

Are we paying to treat illness, or to prevent it?

Mostly to treat it, once people are ill.

Prevention only gets a boost in a crisis.

Does the money reach every state?

Where you live changes how much is spent near you.

Spending is counted where hospitals are, not where patients live. Keep that in mind on these maps.

Does every state get a fair share?

Not evenly. Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya's figures are inflated, as their big hospitals also treat patients from nearby states.

What this means for you

Public money is growing, but not fast enough

  • Care keeps costing more

    Spending per person rose from RM1,237 in 2011 to RM2,637 in 2024. Even after inflation, that is 74% more.

  • The government's share is shrinking

    Public money paid 56% of the bill in 2011, but 51% in 2024. Health also gets a smaller slice of the budget than in Singapore or Thailand.

  • Families fill the gap

    Households now pay 38.8% of the bill, about RM1,020 per person a year. The biggest share goes to private hospitals.

Next time you pay at a clinic or pharmacy, you are part of that 38.8%.
