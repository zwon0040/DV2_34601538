Malaysia spends more on health every year. This page follows the money: who pays, what it buys, and where it goes.

Part 1

The bill keeps growing

Spending per person has more than doubled since 2011.

Is health care getting more expensive?

Yes, for every Malaysian, every year.

And it isn't only rising prices. Even after inflation, far more is spent per person than in 2011.

+74%growth in spending per person after inflation, 2011 to 2024

Was the rise just a COVID-19 effect?

No. The pandemic likely added the extra spending in 2020 and 2021.

When it eased, spending dipped once, then kept growing.

A bill that keeps growing raises the next question: who pays it?

−RM5.6 billionthe only yearly fall since 2019, in 2022 (2024 prices)

Part 2

Who pays the bill?

Families now carry a bigger share of the bill.

Whose money pays for health care?

Mostly two payers: the Ministry of Health and households paying from their own pockets.

Families' spending has grown faster, so they now carry more of the bill.

3×growth in household out-of-pocket spending, 2011 to 2024

Is the burden shifting?

Yes, towards families. Their share keeps rising while the Ministry's slips.

Private insurance has not stepped in to fill the gap.

So what does all this money actually buy?

Part 3

What does the money buy?

Illness care dominates, but COVID-19 shifted priorities.

Are we paying to treat illness, or to prevent it?

Mostly to treat it. Prevention surged only during the 2021 vaccination drive, then fell back.

Meanwhile medicines and buildings kept growing steadily.

RM10.9 billionspent on prevention in 2021, its COVID-19 peak

Has where we get care changed?

Barely. Hospitals, clinics and administration always lead.

Pharmacies are the one steady climber, perhaps as more people buy medicines directly. The one-off spike in labs was likely COVID-19 testing.

Hover over a line to follow one place.

~10×jump in lab, imaging and ambulance spending, 2020 to 2021

Does it matter where you get care?

Yes. At hospitals, the government covers about half the bill.

At the pharmacy, patients pay almost everything themselves.

Wider columns mean more money. Hover for RM values.

6%of pharmacy spending paid by the government in 2024

Where do families' own ringgit go?

Mostly to private hospitals, and that bill has nearly tripled since 2011.

Public care takes only a tiny share, likely because it is heavily subsidised.

Families are paying more. Is the government doing its part?

RM15.3 billionpaid by households at private hospitals in 2024

Part 4

The government's own priorities

The Ministry spends almost all of what it plans.

Does the government spend what it plans?

Almost all of it, so its budget is a good guide to what it does.

Only management left a noticeable amount unspent.

97%of the Ministry's 2025 budget actually spent

What is the government investing in?

People first: salaries are its biggest cost.

Building and equipment budgets grew fastest, partly because hospital support services moved into them in 2020–21.

But does this money reach every state equally?

3.8×growth of the development budget since 2015

Part 5

Does the money reach every state?

Where you live changes how much is spent near you.

Spending is counted where hospitals are, not where patients live. Keep that in mind on these maps.

Does every state get a fair share?

Not evenly. Sabah, one of the most populous states, receives the least per person.

Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya look far ahead, but their big hospitals also treat patients from nearby states.

Hover over a circle for its exact value.

RM1,690per person in Sabah, the lowest in 2024

Does every state have enough beds?

Not Selangor. It has the most beds, but also the most people, so it has the fewest per person.

Its residents may rely on nearby Kuala Lumpur or on private care.

Hover over a circle to see beds per person.

Is the health workforce keeping up?

Almost every state gained staff, and Kuala Lumpur more than doubled.

Putrajaya is the only state that lost staff.

Hover over a state on the map to follow its line.

◇ Read the 2020 values with caution.

Perlis and Perak swing sharply in 2020, then return to trend. The Perlis jump is almost all nurses.

This may be linked to COVID-19 disruption, but it is not confirmed.

How does Malaysia compare with its neighbours?

In the middle. Health became a bigger priority during COVID-19, then fell back.

Singapore and Thailand still give it a much larger share of government spending.

What this means for you

Three things to remember

  • Care costs more every year

    Spending per person more than doubled from 2011 to 2024, and grew 74% after inflation.

  • More comes from your pocket

    Households paid 38.8% of the bill in 2024, up from 31.9%. Their biggest cost is private hospitals.

  • The government is investing

    The Ministry spent 97% of its 2025 budget. The development budget grew fastest.
