Part 1

The bill keeps growing

Spending per person has more than doubled since 2011.

In 2011, Malaysia spent RM1,237 on health for every person.

By 2024 that figure had more than doubled to RM2,637.

Some of the rise is just prices going up. Even after removing inflation, spending per person grew by about 74%.

The jump in 2020 and 2021 mostly reflects COVID-19. Spending dipped in 2022, then kept climbing.

+74%growth in spending per person after inflation, 2011 to 2024

Zoom in on the last five years, in 2024 ringgit so prices are comparable.

Total spending rose from RM70.6 billion in 2019 to RM89.8 billion in 2024.

2020 and 2021 added RM5.2 billion and RM6.8 billion, during COVID-19. Spending then fell by RM5.6 billion in 2022, and rose again by about RM6 billion in each of the next two years.

−RM5.6 bnthe only yearly fall since 2019, in 2022 (2024 prices)

Part 2

Who pays the bill?

Families now carry a bigger share of the bill.

Two payers cover most of the bill: the Ministry of Health and households paying from their own pockets.

In 2024, the Ministry paid RM39.1 billion. Households paid RM34.8 billion.

Since 2011, household spending has tripled. Ministry spending grew 2.4 times. So a bigger share now lands on families.

What do these words mean?
Out of pocket
Money you pay yourself at the counter, not covered by insurance or the government.
Other government
Public bodies besides the Ministry, such as university hospitals, SOCSO and local councils.
Employers and others
Companies paying for staff care, managed-care firms and charities.
3×growth in household out-of-pocket spending, 2011 to 2024

Now compare each payer's share in 2011 and 2024.

The households line climbs by 6.9 points. The Ministry of Health line slips from 45.9% to 43.6%.

Private insurance barely moves, at about 8%.

Part 3

What does the money buy?

Treating illness dominates, but COVID-19 shifted priorities.

Most of the money goes to treating illness: RM56.6 billion in 2024, or 63%.

Each coloured band is a service. The thicker the band, the more money.

Medicines grew from RM2.2 billion to RM8.6 billion. Buildings and equipment grew from RM2.4 billion to RM8.5 billion.

Prevention bulged to RM10.9 billion in 2021, during the national COVID-19 vaccination drive. It fell back to RM5.1 billion by 2024.

RM10.9 bnspent on prevention in 2021, its COVID-19 peak

Where the money is spent has barely changed. Hospitals, clinics and administration held the top three places every year.

The steady climber is pharmacies and medical shops. They moved from 5th to 4th in 2017, and spending there grew four times, from RM1.9 billion to RM7.6 billion.

The one sudden jump came during COVID-19. Labs, imaging and ambulance rose from RM0.5 billion in 2020 to RM4.5 billion in 2021, then fell back to RM0.4 billion.

Hover over a line to follow one place.

~10×jump in lab, imaging and ambulance spending, 2020 to 2021

Who pays depends on where the money is spent.

The government pays 54% of hospital spending and 43% at clinics.

At pharmacies and medical shops, it pays only 6%. Almost all of the RM7.6 billion comes from private sources.

Wider columns mean more money. Hover for RM values.

6%of pharmacy spending paid by the government in 2024

Where do households spend their own money?

Mostly at private hospitals: RM15.3 billion in 2024, nearly three times the 2011 amount.

Private clinics and pharmacies come next. Public providers took only 4.2% of household spending.

Part 4

The government's own priorities

The Ministry spends almost all of what it plans.

Did the Ministry spend what it planned?

In 2025 it spent 97% of its budget. Most programmes used almost all of their money.

Management used the least: 87%.

97%of the Ministry's 2025 budget actually spent

Salaries are the biggest item: about 53% of the Ministry's 2024 budget.

The development budget, for new buildings and equipment, grew fastest. It rose from RM1.6 billion in 2015 to RM6.1 billion in 2024.

Part of that jump is an accounting change. In 2020 and 2021, hospital support services were moved from the running budget into development.

3.8×growth of the development budget since 2015

Part 5

Does the money reach every state?

Where you live changes how much is spent near you.

Spending is counted where hospitals are, not where patients live. Keep that in mind on these maps.

Sabah receives the least: RM1,690 per person.

Kuala Lumpur (RM6,448) and Putrajaya (RM8,279) look far higher.

Their large hospitals also treat patients from nearby states. So these figures overstate spending on their own residents.

Hover over a state to find it in both views.

RM1,690per person in Sabah, the lowest in 2024

Selangor has the most public hospital beds: 7,640.

But it also has the most people. Per 10,000 residents it has only 10.8 beds, the fewest of any state. The national figure is 15.3.

Hover over a circle to see beds per person.

10.8beds per 10,000 people in Selangor

Government health staff increased in every state from 2014 to 2022, except Putrajaya.

Kuala Lumpur more than doubled (+126%). Kelantan rose by 78%.

Hover over a state on the map to follow its line.

◇ Read the 2020 values with caution.

Several states jump or drop in 2020, then return to trend. Perak falls from 11,995 to 6,566.

Perlis jumps from 1,994 to 10,665. Almost all of it is nurses (914 to 9,465). Doctors and dentists stay flat.

The national total still grows smoothly, from 154,177 to 156,639. So staff seem to be counted under the wrong states, not added.

Each year is counted on 31 December, although the source file labels it 1 January. The cause of the 2020 values is not confirmed. They are shown as published.

How does Malaysia compare with its neighbours?

In 2000, health took 4.6% of all government spending. It peaked at 10.1% in 2021, during COVID-19, then fell to 7.9% in 2023.

That puts Malaysia 7th of 11 ASEAN countries, far behind Singapore (17.4%) and Thailand (15.6%).

What this means for you

Three things to remember

  • Care costs more every year

    Spending per person more than doubled from 2011 to 2024, and grew 74% after inflation.

  • More comes from your pocket

    Households paid 38.8% of the bill in 2024, up from 31.9%. Their biggest cost is private hospitals.

  • The government is investing

    The Ministry spent 97% of its 2025 budget. The development budget grew fastest.
